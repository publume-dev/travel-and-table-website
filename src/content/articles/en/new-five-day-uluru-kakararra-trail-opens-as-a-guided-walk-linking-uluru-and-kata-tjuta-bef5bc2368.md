The five-day, 54-kilometer route includes 38 kilometers of newly built path and overnight glamping camps called wiltja. It is the first opportunity for any operator to bring groups of walkers for overnight stays since 1985, the year the park was handed back to the Aṉangu.

For visitors, the practical effect is a set of cultural constraints: Aṉangu Traditional Owner TJ Thomas meets each walking group at three points to provide cultural interpretation, and photography is not permitted at Kata Tjuṯa until day three to protect sacred men's sites.

The reporting refers to permission for four low-impact glamping camps, but describes only three camps along the route, leaving unclear whether a fourth camp exists. It also does not state the walk's cost or booking details.
