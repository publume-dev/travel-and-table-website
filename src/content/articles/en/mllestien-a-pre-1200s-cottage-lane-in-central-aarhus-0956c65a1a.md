Atlas Obscura describes Møllestien as a narrow lane of 28 small, color-washed cottages running about 100 meters through the heart of Aarhus, Denmark; the lane predates the 1200s.

Møllestien is one of the few places in Aarhus where 1800s working-class housing survives intact, with only roughly one house changing hands each year.

Atlas Obscura also records that in 1686 three women from the lane were tried for witchcraft: one died in prison, one was banished, and one was sentenced to be burned alive before the punishment was commuted. In the late 1950s, artists and actors moved into Møllestien, renovated the houses, and campaigned successfully against a private investor's plan to replace seven properties with a parking garage and supermarket.

The same source suggests the lane may follow a path from the Viking Age, but treats that as uncertain. It also provides no practical visitor information, such as access constraints, best times to visit, or suitability for specific travelers.
