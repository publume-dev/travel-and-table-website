In the post-pandemic travel boom to Japan, writer Pico Iyer chose the less-visited Hokuriku region for quiet. He stayed at Kayotei, a 10-suite ryokan in Yamanaka Onsen founded about 50 years ago by Masanori Kamiguchi, which collaborates with about 30 artisans in Ishikawa and Fukui prefectures.

Yamanaka Onsen, a hot-springs resort in Ishikawa Prefecture, has been a pilgrimage site for over 1,300 years but saw only about 36 foreign visitors per day in 2023. Even more striking, Fukui Prefecture received fewer than one foreign visitor for every 600 going to Tokyo in 2023, placing it 46th among Japan's 47 prefectures in international arrivals. This means these areas retain a sense of quiet and tradition, appealing to travelers seeking depth.

Eiheiji, founded in 1244 by Dogen Zenji, is Japan's largest Zen training center with about 80 monks, and offers a guesthouse named Hakujukan across the street. Additionally, Hannah Kirshner, author of 'Water, Wood & Wild Things', has bought a century-old farmhouse in Yamanaka and is turning it into a writing studio and retreat space in collaboration with local ryokans.
