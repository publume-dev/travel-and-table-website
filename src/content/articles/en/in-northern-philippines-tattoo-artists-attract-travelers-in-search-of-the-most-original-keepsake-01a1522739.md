In the village of Buscalan in the northern Philippines, 109-year-old tribal tattoo artist Apo Whang-od attracts travelers in search of the most original keepsake. She has practiced hand-tapped tribal tattooing for over 90 years, using patterns and symbols that represent the natural and spiritual worlds, such as triangles for fertility and health, honeycomb hexagons for strength, and a sun symbol for life.

The Kalinga province receives approximately 40,000 visitors a year, according to the Kalinga Office of Provincial Tourism. Visitors cannot make appointments with Whang-od, and her availability is not guaranteed; her grandnieces and other female students also practice tattooing in the village.

The village of Buscalan is located in the mountainous area of northern Philippines, with limited accessibility, yet travelers still make the journey. Tattoos are not merely decorative but are part of Kalinga culture, representing tribal identity and beliefs.

The article reports that Whang-od was taken to a hospital, but this is based on an overheard conversation and not confirmed by official sources. Additionally, the exact price and booking process for tattoos are not mentioned in the article.
