On August 2, 2027, a total solar eclipse will cross southern Spain, North Africa, and the Arabian Peninsula, with totality lasting up to six minutes and twenty-three seconds near Luxor, Egypt. Egypt's capital, Cairo, lies outside the path of totality and will only experience a partial eclipse.

For travelers, Luxor's airport, hotels, and infrastructure able to handle large numbers of visitors make it a practical base for viewing the eclipse; Cairo, however, is not on the path of totality and therefore offers only a partial view.

In Egypt, the Temple of Abu Simbel, built for Rameses the Great, is aligned so that sunlight illuminates its inner sanctum on February 22 and October 22 each year. This alignment is said to offer a unique backdrop for viewing the eclipse, though the specific illumination dates are based on local knowledge and may not be independently verified.
